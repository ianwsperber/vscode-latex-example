\subsection*{(b)}

\begin{tcolorbox}[
    colback=blue!5,
    colframe=blue!75!black,
    title=\textbf{Problem: Chain Rule in Integration},
    fonttitle=\large,
    arc=2mm
]
    Evaluate the integral using the chain rule (i.e., \(u\)-substitution):

    \begin{align*}
        \int (3x^{2} - 4x)\, e^{\,x^{3} - 2x^{2}} \, \dd x
    \end{align*}

\end{tcolorbox}

We look for an inner function whose derivative appears as a factor.

\begin{align*}
    u & = x^{3} - 2x^{2} \\
    \dd u & = (3x^{2} - 4x)\, \dd x
\end{align*}

The differential \(\dd u\) exactly matches the factor in front of \(e^{u}\), so the integral collapses to:

\begin{align*}
    \int (3x^{2} - 4x)\, e^{\,x^{3} - 2x^{2}} \, \dd x
    & = \int e^{u} \, \dd u \\
    & = e^{u} + C \\
    & = \boxed{e^{\,x^{3} - 2x^{2}} + C}
\end{align*}
